## Abstract
The commercial viability of domestic Direct Lithium Extraction (DLE) from continental oilfield brines depends fundamentally upon capital expenditure avoidance and regulatory compliance under federal clean energy tax codes. Repurposing mature petroleum wellbores eliminates \$4.5M to \$7.0M per well in greenfield drilling CapEx while eliminating unrecoverable plugging and abandonment (P&A) liabilities. Under Internal Revenue Code (IRC) Section 45X, producers qualify for a 10% production tax credit only if battery-grade purity ($>99.2\%$) and zero fugitive methane emissions are empirically established. This paper develops a discounted cash flow technoeconomic model evaluating a 20,000-metric-ton-per-annum (MTPA) lithium hydroxide facility in the Smackover Formation. We demonstrate that wellbore repurposing reduces the Levelized Cost of Lithium (LCOL) from \$8,200/ton to \$4,350/ton, yielding an internal rate of return (IRR) of $28.4\%$ under current AOGC statutory royalty dockets.

**Keywords:** Levelized Cost of Lithium, Inflation Reduction Act Section 45X, Technoeconomic Analysis, Wellbore Repurposing, Critical Mineral Policy.

## 1. Technoeconomic Cash Flow Model
The Levelized Cost of Lithium ($\text{LCOL}$) is evaluated over a 25-year operational lifecycle:

$$\text{LCOL} = \frac{\sum_{t=0}^{N} \frac{\text{CapEx}_t + \text{OpEx}_t - \text{Credit}_{\text{45X}, t}}{(1 + r)^t}}{\sum_{t=0}^{N} \frac{M_{\text{LiOH}, t}}{(1 + r)^t}}$$

Where $r = 8\%$ discount rate, $\text{CapEx}_0 = \$145\text{M}$ (reflecting $52\%$ wellbore drilling avoidance across 24 repurposed well pairs), and $\text{Credit}_{\text{45X}} = 0.10 \times \text{OpEx}$.
