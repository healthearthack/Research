## Abstract
Direct Lithium Extraction (DLE) from deep continental aquifers produces massive volumes of formation brine at elevated temperatures ($120–145^\circ\text{C}$). While commercial layered double hydroxide (LDH) and ion-exchange sorbents suffer severe structural degradation and loss of selectivity at temperatures exceeding $80^\circ\text{C}$, conventional surface facilities deploy passive cooling towers that waste immense thermal energy. This paper models the thermodynamic integration of subcritical binary Organic Rankine Cycles (ORC) directly upstream of DLE adsorption columns. Utilizing environmentally benign working fluids (R1233zd(E)), the system extracts $850\text{ kW}$ of net electrical power from a single 14,000 bpd well stream while cooling the brine to the optimal $65^\circ\text{C}$ sorption window. This self-powered architecture offsets 100% of surface reinjection pumping parasitic loads, reducing levelized operational extraction costs under U.S. Inflation Reduction Act provisions.

**Keywords:** Low-Enthalpy Geothermal, Organic Rankine Cycle, Direct Lithium Extraction, Thermodynamic Modeling, Net-Zero Skids.

## 1. Thermodynamic Cycle Formulation
The first-law thermal efficiency $\eta_{\text{th}}$ of the binary ORC evaporator is formulated:

$$\dot{W}_{\text{net}} = \dot{m}_{\text{wf}} \left( (h_1 - h_2) - (h_4 - h_3) \right)$$

$$\eta_{\text{th}} = \frac{\dot{W}_{\text{net}}}{\dot{Q}_{\text{in}}} = \frac{\dot{W}_{\text{turbine}} - \dot{W}_{\text{pump}}}{\dot{m}_{\text{brine}} c_p (T_{\text{in}} - T_{\text{out}})}$$

Where $\dot{m}_{\text{brine}} = 26.2\text{ kg/s}$, $T_{\text{in}} = 140^\circ\text{C}$, and $T_{\text{out}} = 65^\circ\text{C}$, yielding over $850\text{ kW}_{\text{e}}$ of continuous base-load power.
