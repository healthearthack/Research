# Heriot-Watt University: Institute of GeoEnergy Engineering (IGE) Profile

**Institution:** Heriot-Watt University, Edinburgh Campus, Scotland, UK  
**School:** School of Energy, Geoscience, Infrastructure and Society (EGIS)  
**Research Entity:** Institute of GeoEnergy Engineering (IGE)  
**Strategic Research Focus:** Subsurface Energy Transition, Critical Minerals, & Digital Subsurface  

---

## 1. Institutional Legacy & Strategic Positioning
The Institute of GeoEnergy Engineering (IGE) at Heriot-Watt University (formerly the Institute of Petroleum Engineering - IPE) is internationally recognized as the preeminent academic institution for subsurface fluid mechanics, reservoir engineering, and the geo-energy transition. 

Located in Edinburgh, Scotland, IGE leads global research in:
* **The GeoEnergy Transition:** Repurposing oil and gas subsurface assets for carbon capture and storage (CCS), hydrogen storage, and deep geothermal systems.
* **Critical Minerals & Unconventional Brines:** Direct Lithium Extraction (DLE) from high-enthalpy formation brines.
* **Digital Geoscience & Data Integration:** Real-time downhole telemetry, distributed fiber-optic sensing, and physics-informed machine learning for reservoir operations.

---

## 2. Advanced Facilities & Research Capabilities
* **Centre for Energy Transition:** Interdisciplinary research hub accelerating net-zero subsurface technologies.
* **High-Pressure / High-Temperature (HPHT) Core Flood Laboratories:** World-leading experimental facilities capable of simulating reservoir pressure, fluid-rock interaction, and multiphase transport.
* **Micro-CT & Digital Rock Physics Lab:** 3D pore-scale imaging and fluid displacement visualization.
* **Computing Clusters:** High-performance computing (HPC) nodes dedicated to reservoir simulation, edge data processing, and numerical fluid dynamics.

---

## 3. Supervision & Methodological Intersection
Doctoral research bridging **Subsurface Fluid Mechanics, DLE, and Cyber-Physical Telemetry Security** directly maps to IGE's core research groups:
1. **Multiphase Fluid Flow & Transport in Porous Media:** Providing the physical governing equations (Darcy's Law, Navier-Stokes) for brine and lithium extraction.
2. **Geothermal & Hydrothermal Energy Systems:** Thermally coupled fluid models for coproduction of heat and battery feedstocks.
3. **Subsurface Monitoring & Telemetry Infrastructure:** Integration of Distributed Acoustic Sensing (DAS) and operational technology (OT) protocols to detect pressure anomalies and secure remote wellhead chokes.
